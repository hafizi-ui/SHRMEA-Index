Findings & publications

What the study found

Sustainable HRM was strong at the strategic and reporting levels but fragmented in the internal culture on the floor. Every firm’s management rated itself higher than its employees did.

Summary of findings

Four objectives, four answers

RO1

Employer level

Employers built sustainability into compliance-driven reporting and four practice pillars: Green HRM, Socially Responsible HRM, Triple Bottom Line HRM and Common Good HRM. The last two were applied inconsistently.

RO2

Employee level

Employees recognised transparent reporting and practices such as green waste management and welfare benefits. They also reported resistance and disengagement driven by cost constraints, decarbonisation pressure and limited awareness.

RO3

Misalignment across cases

A persistent strategic disconnect. Employer commitment to reporting and sustainability KPIs was undermined by internal culture, leading to inconsistent training, unequal rewards and weak employee buy-in in all three firms.

RO4

The SHRMEA framework

A new diagnostic tool that turns the employer–employee “say–do” gap into a measurable alignment score across 16 indicators, comparable between firms and trackable over time.

Alignment results

No firm has reached full convergence yet

The sector average is 52.6%. In every firm the employer score is higher than the employee score, so higher alignment comes from rising employer effort rather than from employees catching up.

Where misalignment comes from

A failure of transmission, not of goals

Employers and employees both value sustainability. What breaks down are the structures that should carry employer intent into employee practice. Training dominates the 48 coded instances of misalignment.

The return path from employees back to employers is also broken. Few employees read or contribute to the sustainability report, so management never receives the signal that would reveal the gap.

Practical implications

A multilevel action framework for firms

Macro · firm

Institutionalise participation

Give employees clear roles in waste, reporting, CSR and energy, and set up sustainability committees with real decision-making power.

Meso · HR system

Link KPIs to performance

Cascade firm-wide sustainability KPIs down to team and individual targets, backed by rewards and recognition.

Micro · employee

Deepen training

Move from passive compliance briefings to active, problem-solving learning delivered consistently across plants and levels.

Culture

Share ownership

Tell the story through employee examples and feedback loops so sustainability becomes a source of pride, not just a compliance obligation.
