SHRMEA Framework

The Sustainable Human Resource Management Employer–Employee Alignment framework measures the gap between what employers commit to on sustainability and what employees actually experience on the floor. It shows where that gap is and what to do about it.

What is SHRMEA?

Sustainability policy only works if it reaches the floor

Many firms publish sustainability reports, set net-zero targets and run compulsory training. Those commitments are real, but employees often experience them very differently: training without buy-in, reports nobody reads, and KPIs that lose out to production speed.

SHRMEA puts the employer (ER) and employee (EE) views side by side on the same 16 indicators. Where the two views disagree is an audit finding. It is a perception gap that shows exactly where policy is not reaching the workforce.

The framework was developed in a PhD study at Universiti Malaysia Pahang Al-Sultan Abdullah (UMPSA), grounded in Signalling Theory and Ability–Motivation–Opportunity (AMO) theory.

How alignment is scored

A
Achieved = 1
Fully in place; intent and practice evidenced
P
Partial = 0.5
Exists but inconsistent; policy–practice gap
X
Not achieved = 0
Absent, or a clear contradiction

Alignment % = (ER score + EE score) ÷ 2 ÷ 16 × 100. Firms are then placed on a maturity stage from 1 to 7, and the widest gap pillar is flagged as “Fix First”.

Research objectives

What the study set out to do

The main question: how do sustainable HRM practices shape employers’ commitments and employees’ experiences in line with the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)?

RO1

Employer alignment with the SDGs

Explore how far firms align their HR operations with the Sustainable Development Goals.

RO2

Employee experience

Evaluate how employees actually experience sustainable HRM practices.

RO3

Differences across firms

Determine how sustainable HRM, and its misalignments, differ between types of firms.

RO4

Build the SHRMEA framework

Develop an employer–employee alignment framework that measures these misalignments.

Two ways to use SHRMEA

Compare across firms, or look inside one firm

The Assessment Tool has two modules. Both use the same 16 indicators, the same A / P / X scoring and the same four gap pillars.

Module 1 · Firm level

Cross Firm Analysis (Employer–Employee)

Checks whether employer intent reaches employees at the firm level, and how firms compare with each other.

Compares
Several firms, sites or subsidiaries (up to 10 at once)
Who rates
The employer view (ER) and the employee view (EE) of each firm
Typical user
Top management, ESG officers, auditors, consultants and researchers
Key outputs
Alignment %, maturity stage (1–7), signed ER − EE gaps, spider profiles, firm ranking and a Fix-First pillar per firm
Module 2 · Department level

Cross Department Analysis

Benchmarks the departments of one firm against each other, to find weak units and firm-wide hotspots.

Compares
The departments of one firm (names are editable), such as Production, Maintenance, QA and HR
Who rates
One A / P / X rating per department on each of the 16 indicators
Typical user
HR business partners, plant managers and heads of department
Key outputs
Maturity % and stage per department, ranking, firm-wide average, spread and consistency, strongest and weakest department, indicator hotspots, Fix-First and systemic pillars, a department × indicator heatmap, spider charts and action cards
How it works

From ratings to one action plan

Step 1

Rate the indicators

Pick a module. For each firm, record the employer view (ER) and the employee view (EE). For each department, record one rating. Both use the same 16 indicators and A / P / X.

Step 2

Measure and compare

For firms, the tool calculates the alignment %, maturity stage, signed ER − EE gaps and spider profiles. For departments, it calculates maturity % and stage, ranks the departments, and shows how even maturity is across the firm and which indicators are weak in most departments.

Step 3

Fix the weakest pillar

Results are grouped into Knowledge, Incentive, Resource and Value pillars. The widest gap for a firm, or the weakest pillar for a department, becomes the first action. When most departments share the same one, it is flagged as systemic and fixed at firm level.
