About the research

The Development of a Sustainable HRM Employer–Employee Alignment Framework

A PhD study (Doctor of Philosophy, Faculty of Industrial Management, UMPSA, 2026) on why sustainability commitments made at the top of manufacturing firms are not always felt by the people who carry them out.

Background

Why this study

Malaysia’s 12th Malaysia Plan targets carbon neutrality by 2050, and Bursa Malaysia requires listed firms to publish sustainability reports. Human resource management is central to meeting these goals, because environmental, social and economic targets depend on people’s behaviour, skills and culture.

Manufacturing is an ideal setting. Factories use a lot of energy, produce a lot of waste and rely on labour-intensive processes. Yet research on sustainable HRM (SHRM) still focuses mostly on the organisation and gives little attention to the employee’s lived experience.

Firms send sustainability “signals” through policy, reports and training. Employees may read those signals differently. When a firm promotes sustainability while workers feel pressure and burnout, the result is disengagement. This study set out to measure that gap.

Research gaps

Three gaps addressed

Theoretical gap

Few multilevel studies. Most SHRM research collects data at several levels but analyses only one. Response: a multilevel qualitative study.

Empirical gap

Limited employee perspectives, and an over-reliance on sustainability reports. Response: in-depth employee interviews.

Contextual gap

Limited Malaysian and Asian manufacturing evidence. Response: manufacturing case studies in Malaysia.

Theoretical foundation

Two theories, four SHRM practice pillars

The framework treats sustainable HRM as a signalling process between two organisational levels.

Signalling Theory

Employers transmit sustainability signals through policy, disclosure and formal structures. Employees receive and interpret those signals, sometimes very differently from what was intended.

Ability–Motivation–Opportunity (AMO)

Whether employees act on those signals depends on their ability (skills and training), motivation (incentives and rewards) and opportunity (participation and involvement).

GHRM

Green HRM

HR policies that reduce the environmental harm of firm practices.

SRHRM

Socially Responsible HRM

CSR focused on employees and its effective implementation.

TBLHRM

Triple Bottom Line HRM

Balancing people, planet and profit.

CGHRM

Common Good HRM

Common-good practices across HR policy, practice and structure, reaching supply chains and communities.

The instrument

16 indicators in 4 dimensions

Each indicator is rated twice, once from the employer (ER) view and once from the employee (EE) view, using A = 1, P = 0.5 and X = 0.

#Sustainability indicatorGap pillar
Diagnosis

Four gap pillars: where policy fails to land

To make the result actionable, the 16 indicators are also grouped into four gap pillars. Each firm gets a score on every pillar (the average |ER − EE| gap), and the widest pillar is flagged as Fix First.

Research paradigm

Interpretivism with a qualitative approach, exploring the “how” and “why” of SHRM implementation (Yin, 2018).

Case selection

Purposive sampling. Firms needed formal sustainability policies or reporting, a sustainability mandate within HR, at least 4 years of operation, a Bursa Malaysia listing, and access to managers and employees.

Informants

Senior management, HR professionals and middle managers (employer level), plus employees (employee level). Interviews were pre-tested before full data collection.

Deductive–inductive analysis

Three coding levels: codes (first cycle), categories (second cycle) and themes. Analysis ran within each case and then across cases, with memo writing throughout.

Trustworthiness

Validity, reliability and trustworthiness procedures were applied to ensure the credibility of the qualitative findings.

The case firms

The study firms represent different manufacturing industries operating in Malaysia.

Extending the framework

Two lenses: across firms and across departments

The Assessment Tool applies the SHRMEA indicators in two ways. The firm lens is the thesis model. The department lens is an extension of the tool built on the same indicators; it is not a thesis finding.

Lens 1 · Across firms

Employer–employee alignment

Each firm is rated twice, from the employer (ER) and the employee (EE) view. The gap between the two shows whether employer commitments reach the workforce, and how firms compare. This is the model developed and tested in the thesis.

Lens 2 · Across departments

Benchmarking departments of one firm

Each department gets one A / P / X rating on the same 16 indicators. Departments are then compared with each other to find weak units and firm-wide hotspots, so that improvement effort goes where it is needed most.

Why a department lens? A firm-level score can hide large differences inside the firm. Sustainable HRM may be well established in one department and barely present in another. Comparing departments on the same indicators shows whether sustainability maturity is even across the firm or depends on the unit.

The department lens keeps the numbering, themes, gap pillars and A / P / X scoring of the firm lens. Only the wording of each indicator is adapted to the department, as in the examples below.

What the department analysis shows

  • Maturity and ranking. A maturity % and stage for each department, a ranking and a firm-wide average.
  • Spread and consistency. The gap between the strongest and weakest department shows whether maturity is Consistent, Variable or Fragmented across the firm.
  • Indicator hotspots. Indicators that are weak in at least half of the departments. They point to firm-wide problems rather than local ones.
  • Fix First and systemic pillars. Each department’s weakest pillar is its Fix First. When most departments share the same one, it is flagged as systemic and needs firm-level action.

SHRMEA converts a qualitative “say–do” gap into a measurable alignment score. That score can be compared across firms and tracked over time.
